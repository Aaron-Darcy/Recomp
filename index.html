Recomp

Calories planned around your training, a weight trend that ignores daily noise, and a weekly check-in that adjusts itself. Everything stays in this browser.

Tap what you're doing each day. Skipping a session this week? Turn it off and that day's target drops.

Past check-ins

Calories are pre-filled with that day's target. If you roughly hit it, leave it. If you went well over or under, put in your best guess. Recomp compares this with your weight trend to learn your real maintenance calories. Activity ticks fill in automatically from Strava and Strong.

      Excel backup

      Pick a file once and every change saves to it automatically (Chrome or Edge). Open it in Excel to browse your log, weekly summary, check-ins and next-session targets. If you edit weights or calories in Excel, Restore from Excel brings those edits back in.

      Strava (runs & sport from your watch)

      Anything that reaches Strava can be pulled in: Apple Watch, Garmin, Coros, Wear OS and so on. Apple Watch: in the Strava iPhone app go to You → ⚙ Settings → Applications, Services, and Devices → Health and allow workout uploads.

      Apple Health (heart, sleep, food, workouts)

      Health Auto Export on your iPhone sends your Apple Health data in the background to your own small relay, and Recomp collects it from there, just like Hevy and Strava. One-off setup, about 10 minutes.

      Hevy (gym sessions)

      Hevy Pro has an official API, so your workouts sync automatically. Get your API key at hevy.com/settings?developer and paste it below. It's stored only in this browser and never written to backups.

      Morning weigh-in from your iPhone (one tap, no typing into Recomp)
      1. In the Shortcuts app make a shortcut with two actions: Ask for Input (type: Number, prompt "Weight (kg)?") and Log Health Sample (Weight = Provided Input, kg).
      2. In Hevy, let it read your weight from Apple Health. Hevy then copies each weigh-in into its Measures, and Recomp picks it up from Hevy.
      3. Run it from a Lock Screen or Control Center button, Back Tap, an Apple Watch complication, or Automation → Alarm → Is Stopped, so it pops up as you get up.

      Hevy grabs new Health weights when it syncs (usually when you open the Hevy app). Recomp checks Hevy every time you open it or come back to the tab. The shortcut holds no API key, so there's nothing to leak.

      Strong (CSV import)

      Strong can export your workouts: Strong → Profile → ⚙ Settings → Export Strong Data. Get the CSV onto this computer and import it. Your full history feeds the Lifts tab and dashboard. Only sessions from your start date onwards get ticked in the daily log. Re-importing only updates, never duplicates.

      Plain backup

      How the numbers are worked out

      1. Base burn: your BMR (Mifflin–St Jeor formula) × your daily-life factor. That covers everything except training.
      2. Plus training: each gym session, sport session, match and run distance adds its own calories (change them above, or use your watch's averages from Strava).
      3. Plus learnt adjustment: after about 2 weeks of weigh-ins and calorie entries, Recomp compares what you ate with what your weight actually did, and corrects the formula. It's locked in at each weekly check-in so targets don't drift mid-week.
      4. Plus your goal: a daily deficit (cut), nothing (maintain) or a surplus (bulk).

      Getting clean data

      Weigh yourself every morning (or as often as you can) after the toilet, before food or drink, in the same clothes. Daily weight bounces ±0.5–1 kg from water, salt and carbs, so only watch the trend line. The first week of a new calorie level often shows a jump or drop from water and stored carbs. That isn't fat.

      Rough rates

      ~7700 kcal ≈ 1 kg of body weight change. A 250 kcal/day surplus is roughly +0.2 kg/week; a 500 kcal/day deficit roughly −0.45 kg/week. The check-in tells you whether you're on pace and what to change.
